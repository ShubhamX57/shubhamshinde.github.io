Numerical Stability of Covariance-Matrix Inversion in kinisi
University of Bristol · supervised by the library's author, Dr Andrew McCluskey
2025 – present

kinisi extracts diffusion coefficients from molecular-dynamics trajectories. It does something unusually careful: it propagates the full covariance matrix through its fitting, rather than treating variance estimates at different lag times as independent. That is harder, slower - and the direct cause of the fragility I was asked to investigate. Its matrices were collapsing into non-positive-definite states, which silently degrades fitted coefficients and their uncertainties without raising any error.

Rather than patch it, I built a controlled testbed with known analytical ground truth, so numerical artifact could be separated from real physical signal. Everything downstream depends on that control existing.

Generalised least squares - requires $\Sigma^{-1}$
$$\hat{\beta} = \left(X^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}X\right)^{-1}X^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}y$$
$\Sigma$ = MSD covariance · inversion fails when $\lambda_{\min}\!\le\!0$
Upstream library
github.com/bjmorgan/kinisi
What the investigation found
  • Matrix failure rate fell from 90% → 2% as the atom count increased - the matrices were under-determined, not wrong
  • Root cause isolated to sample noise in long-lag-time variance estimates, via a systematic convergence study
  • Reconditioning strategies (minimum-eigenvalue, ridge, linear & non-linear shrinkage) benchmarked against the analytical matrix
  • A data-adaptive eigenvalue floor improved parameter recovery over the library's fixed-threshold default
Fig. 2   Eigenvalue spectrum: trailing modes collapse below the floor under low sampling
Status - stated honestly
  • Results hold on a controlled random-walk testbed; validation across noise levels is ongoing
  • The adaptive rule is proposed and tested offline; the contribution is not yet merged upstream

QENS Analysis & Visualisation Library
Author · methodology aligned with ISIS Neutron Source workflows
2025 – present

An open-source library for quasi-elastic neutron scattering analysis that I designed and wrote. It implements the full physics chain from raw HDF5 files through resolution correction, model fitting, and Bayesian parameter estimation, surfaced through a FastAPI + React interface.

The core model is the Chudley–Elliott jump-diffusion framework, describing how molecules hop between sites in a liquid or confined geometry. Getting the resolution-function deconvolution right was the decisive challenge - a naive approach inflates the diffusion coefficient substantially.

Chudley–Elliott structure factor
$$S(Q,\omega) = \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(Q)}{\omega^2 + \Gamma^2(Q)}$$
where $\Gamma(Q)=\dfrac{D\,Q^2}{1+Q^2 l^2}$ - $D$ = diffusion coefficient, $l$ = jump length
Resolution-corrected measured spectrum
$$I(Q,\omega) = S(Q,\omega)\otimes R(Q,\omega) + B(Q)$$
$R(Q,\omega)$ = instrumental resolution · $B(Q)$ = background
Key results
  • ~16% reduction in systematic error on the diffusion coefficient via physics-correct resolution correction
  • 3× better χ² goodness-of-fit for Chudley–Elliott vs a Fickian diffusion model
  • Bayesian MCMC across 4 independent chains, convergence verified at Gelman–Rubin $\hat{R}<1.01$
  • Calibrated uncertainties on fitted parameters, not bare point estimates

Lebwohl–Lasher Accelerated
Performance engineering study · seven implementations, one physics
2025

A Monte Carlo simulation of the Lebwohl–Lasher liquid-crystal model, taken through seven progressively faster implementations - pure Python, NumPy vectorisation, Numba, Cython, Cython + OpenMP, MPI, and hybrid MPI + Cython - reaching roughly 4.7× the baseline.

The part I cared about most was not the speedup but the cross-version consistency tests: every optimised implementation is verified to reproduce the reference physics. A faster wrong answer is worse than a slow right one, and optimisation is exactly where that failure hides.

Benchmarked systematically across lattice size, Monte Carlo step count, temperature and process count, to find where parallelisation overheads start to dominate rather than assuming they scale.

ArXiv RAG - retrieval with adversarial evaluation
Personal project · 204 papers indexed · 224 tests · CI on every push
2026

A retrieval-augmented generation system over ArXiv machine-learning papers. What started as a retrieval exercise became a question about trust.

During smoke testing I asked it a question with no answer in the corpus, expecting abstention. Instead it answered confidently from the model's own parametric knowledge and attached a real citation to a chunk that did not support the claim. The striking part was not the hallucination, which is expected - it was that the citation made the failure look like success. Every surface signal said the system was working.

So I built a deterministic defence rather than a prompt-based one: verbatim quote verification against the source chunk, with forced abstention when no citation survives. Then, because a defence you have not tried to break is not a defence, I built an adversarial evaluation set and measured it.

Open source repository
github.com/ShubhamX57/arxiv-rag
Fig. 5   Verification gate - an answer ships only if a citation survives verbatim checking
Measured, including what didn't work
  • Defence held even when retrieval was deliberately steered into the trap chunk - verification checks real chunk text, not the model's reasoning about it
  • Multi-query expansion: no improvement
  • HyDE: −5 pp, actively harmful for this corpus
  • Both negative results published in the repository, with limitations documented
