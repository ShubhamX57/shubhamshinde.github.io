Numerical stability of covariance matrix inversion in kinisi

University of Bristol, supervised by the library's author, Dr Andrew McCluskey

kinisi extracts diffusion coefficients from molecular dynamics trajectories. It does something unusually careful: it propagates the full covariance matrix through its fitting rather than treating variance estimates at different lag times as independent. That is harder, slower, and the direct cause of the fragility I was asked to investigate. The matrices were collapsing into non positive definite states, which silently degrades fitted coefficients and their uncertainties without raising any error.

Rather than patch it, I built a controlled testbed with known analytical ground truth, so that numerical artefact could be separated from real physical signal. Everything downstream depends on that control existing.

Generalised least squares, which requires the inverse covariance
$$\hat{\beta} = \left(X^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}X\right)^{-1}X^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}y$$
Σ is the MSD covariance. Inversion fails when the smallest eigenvalue reaches zero.

What the investigation found

  • Matrix failure rate fell from 90% to 2% as the atom count increased, showing the matrices were under-determined rather than wrong.
  • Root cause isolated to sample noise in long lag time variance estimates, through a systematic convergence study.
  • Reconditioning strategies benchmarked against the analytical matrix: minimum eigenvalue, ridge, linear and non-linear shrinkage.
  • A data-adaptive eigenvalue floor improved parameter recovery over the library's fixed threshold default.

Current status

  • Results hold on a controlled random walk testbed. Validation across noise levels is ongoing.
  • The adaptive rule is proposed and tested offline. The contribution is not yet merged upstream.

QENS analysis and visualisation library

Methodology aligned with ISIS Neutron Source workflows

An open source library for quasi-elastic neutron scattering analysis that I designed and wrote. It implements the full physics chain from raw HDF5 files through resolution correction, model fitting, and Bayesian parameter estimation, surfaced through a FastAPI and React interface.

The core model is Chudley-Elliott jump diffusion, describing how molecules hop between sites in a liquid or a confined geometry. Getting the resolution function deconvolution right was the decisive challenge, since a naive approach inflates the diffusion coefficient substantially.

Chudley-Elliott structure factor
$$S(Q,\omega) = \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(Q)}{\omega^2 + \Gamma^2(Q)}, \qquad \Gamma(Q)=\frac{D\,Q^2}{1+Q^2 l^2}$$
D is the diffusion coefficient and l the jump length.
Resolution corrected measured spectrum
$$I(Q,\omega) = S(Q,\omega)\otimes R(Q,\omega) + B(Q)$$
R is the instrumental resolution and B the background.

Results

  • Roughly 16% reduction in systematic error on the diffusion coefficient through physics-correct resolution correction.
  • 3 times better χ² goodness of fit for Chudley-Elliott against a Fickian diffusion model.
  • Bayesian MCMC across 4 independent chains, convergence verified at Gelman-Rubin R̂ below 1.01.
  • Calibrated uncertainties on every fitted parameter rather than bare point estimates.

Lebwohl-Lasher accelerated

Seven implementations, one set of physics

A Monte Carlo simulation of the Lebwohl-Lasher liquid crystal model, taken through seven progressively faster implementations: pure Python, NumPy vectorisation, Numba, Cython, Cython with OpenMP, MPI, and hybrid MPI with Cython, reaching roughly 4.7 times the baseline.

The part I cared about most was not the speedup but the cross-version consistency tests. Every optimised implementation is verified to reproduce the reference physics. A faster wrong answer is worse than a slow right one, and optimisation is exactly where that failure hides.

Benchmarks sweep lattice size, Monte Carlo step count, temperature, and process count, to find where parallelisation overhead starts to dominate rather than assuming it scales.

Engineering emphasis

  • Cross-version consistency tests confirm identical physics at every optimisation level.
  • Parameter sweeps across lattice size, step count, temperature, and process count.
  • Interactive dashboard published for inspecting and comparing runs.

ArXiv RAG, retrieval with adversarial evaluation

204 papers indexed, 224 tests, CI on every push

A retrieval augmented generation system over ArXiv machine learning papers. What started as a retrieval exercise became a question about trust.

During smoke testing I asked it a question with no answer in the corpus, expecting abstention. Instead it answered confidently from the model's own parametric knowledge and attached a real citation to a chunk that did not support the claim. The striking part was not the hallucination, which is expected. It was that the citation made the failure look like success. Every surface signal said the system was working.

So I built a deterministic defence rather than a prompt based one: verbatim quote verification against the source chunk, with forced abstention when no citation survives. Then, because a defence you have not tried to break is not a defence, I built an adversarial evaluation set and measured it.

Measured, including what did not work

  • The defence held even when retrieval was deliberately steered into the trap chunk, because verification checks real chunk text rather than the model's reasoning about it.
  • Multi-query expansion gave no improvement.
  • HyDE cost 5 percentage points and was actively harmful for this corpus.
  • Both negative results are published in the repository, with limitations documented.
